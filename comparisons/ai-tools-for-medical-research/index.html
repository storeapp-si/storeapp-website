Medical AI and research tools: separate clinical support from literature review

OpenEvidence, AMBOSS, Elicit and Scite serve different users and tasks. A clinical information workflow is not the same as exam preparation or systematic literature discovery. This guide separates those use cases and sets out a safer evaluation method.

Editorial method: This is a task-based decision guide, not a controlled benchmark or universal ranking. Product capabilities, access and pricing can change. Confirm current details with each vendor and test on your own representative workflow.

The short answer

OpenEvidence

Evaluate as a clinical evidence and medical question-support resource, subject to its access rules, supported regions and source verification.

AMBOSS

Evaluate for structured medical learning, reference material and exam-oriented study workflows.

Elicit

Evaluate for finding, screening and synthesising research papers and extracting information from studies.

Scite

Evaluate when you need to inspect how publications are cited, including whether later work supports or disputes a claim.

Match the tool to the task

Clinical question

Check whether the answer links to current, relevant primary evidence and whether population, date, guideline and contraindications are explicit.

Learning and revision

Test curriculum coverage, explanation quality, question feedback and whether references align with the learner's syllabus.

Literature review

Use a fixed research question and known seed papers. Measure relevant-paper recall, screening effort, extraction accuracy and reproducibility.

Citation context

Inspect the underlying citing papers. A citation label alone does not prove that a source supports the proposition.

Evaluation and safety

  • Check access eligibility, country availability, institutional licensing and the currency of medical content.
  • Do not enter identifiable patient data unless the organisation has approved the service and its data-processing terms.
  • Verify recommendations against primary literature, current local guidelines and qualified clinicians.
  • For research, record search dates, databases, inclusion criteria and human screening decisions.

Important limitation

  • These tools do not replace clinical judgement, diagnosis, prescribing or institutional protocols. AI summaries can omit context or misrepresent evidence. Patient-care decisions remain with appropriately qualified healthcare professionals.
