AI for engineering simulation: compare the workflow before the model

AI-assisted simulation products can accelerate parts of an engineering workflow, but their value depends on the physics, training data, boundary conditions and validation process. Ansys SimAI and Siemens Simcenter AI should be assessed against the same engineering problem—not a generic speed claim.

Editorial method: This is a task-based decision guide, not a controlled benchmark or universal ranking. Product capabilities, access and pricing can change. Confirm current details with each vendor and test on your own representative workflow.

What to compare

Physics and use case

Define the exact discipline, geometry range, materials, operating conditions and outputs. A model trained for one design space may not generalise to another.

Training data

Check data volume, quality, coverage, provenance and whether the tool exposes limitations outside the training domain.

Accuracy and uncertainty

Compare predictions with held-out high-fidelity simulations and physical measurements. Report error distributions, not a single best-case example.

Engineering integration

Measure setup effort, compatibility with existing solvers, CAD/CAE data, version control and reproducibility.

Pilot protocol

  • Select one bounded engineering problem with a trusted baseline solver and known validation cases.
  • Separate training, validation and test cases to avoid data leakage.
  • Measure prediction error by operating regime and identify out-of-domain inputs.
  • Keep a conventional solver or physical test as the acceptance reference for safety-critical decisions.

Important limitation

  • An AI surrogate is not automatically a replacement for validated physics-based simulation. Never use unvalidated predictions as the sole basis for safety-critical design, certification or release decisions.
