DeepL vs Google Translate: Which Is Better for Your Needs?

Both services can translate everyday text, but they suit different workflows. Compare the exact language pair, document requirements, terminology controls, device features and privacy terms before choosing.

Arabic, English and French: a practical language-pair test

For readers who work across Arabic, English and French, the most useful question is not which translator wins overall. It is how each service handles your exact direction, register, terminology and regional variety. DeepL lists Arabic, English and French among its supported languages; its Arabic variety is Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). Google Translate also lists Arabic, English and French, but feature availability can differ by language and mode.

Which one should you use?

Travel and quick conversations

Start with Google Translate if you need mobile camera, speech or offline functions. Confirm that your language pair and downloaded language packs support the feature.

Lean toward: Google Translate for mobile convenience, where supported.

Business writing and recurring terminology

Try DeepL if its language pair works for you and you need glossary, style or formality controls. Review the plan details before relying on these tools.

Lean toward: DeepL for terminology-led workflows, subject to testing.

Long documents

Compare supported file formats, maximum size, document allowances and whether the translated file retains the layout you need.

Choose: The service that handles your actual document with less correction work.

Rare or less common languages

Check both language lists and test the exact direction. Broad language coverage can be decisive when one service does not support your pair.

Choose: The service that supports the pair and produces the more accurate reviewed output.

Legal, medical or safety-critical content

Use machine translation only as an aid or draft. Have a qualified translator or subject-matter professional verify meaning, terminology and omissions.

Required: Qualified human review, regardless of tool.

Developers and high-volume workflows

Compare DeepL API and Google Cloud Translation separately from the free consumer websites. Review API language support, authentication, billing, quotas, data terms and integration needs.

Choose: After a technical pilot and cost estimate.

How to compare translation quality fairly

A meaningful test should use the same source text, language direction and review criteria for both services.

  1. Prepare 10–20 short samples from the content you really translate: everyday messages, idioms, technical terms, names and numbers.
  2. Translate each sample in both services using the same source and target languages.
  3. Remove product names from the outputs and ask a fluent reviewer to compare them without knowing which service produced each translation, if practical.
  4. Record meaning errors, omissions, added information, terminology consistency, tone and readability.
  5. Repeat with one representative document if document translation is important to your workflow.
  6. Choose based on error severity and the time needed to review and correct the output—not on which result merely sounds smoother.

For high-impact material, a fluent reviewer should assess the source and translation. A polished machine translation can still contain a serious error.

Documents: an important difference

Google’s current help documentation says its desktop web translator accepts .docx, .pdf, .pptx and .xlsx files up to 10 MB, with PDFs limited to 300 pages. It also notes that document translation is unavailable on smaller screens or mobile, and text inside images or scanned PDF pages is not translated.

DeepL’s document support depends on the plan and format. Its help center lists different file-size and character allowances across free accounts, Pro subscriptions and API plans. Some paid workflows also add editing and customization options.

Before choosing, check the latest provider instructions: Google’s document translation guide ↗ and DeepL’s supported file formats ↗.

Privacy and business use

Do not treat all versions of a service as having identical data protections. DeepL’s privacy policy distinguishes its free services from Pro products. For Pro translation and writing, it says submitted texts and documents are kept temporarily as needed to provide the service and are not permanently stored or used to train or improve its models. Read the current policy and terms for the exact product and account type.

For Google Translate, review Google’s current privacy terms and the terms for the specific product you use. Consumer Translate and Google Cloud Translation are different products; do not assume that consumer terms, API terms and enterprise controls are interchangeable.

Sources: DeepL Privacy Policy ↗ · Google Privacy Policy ↗.

Frequently asked questions

Is DeepL better than Google Translate?

Not for every language pair or task. DeepL may suit workflows that need glossaries and document controls; Google Translate may suit broad everyday and mobile translation. Test the exact language direction and have a fluent reviewer assess important content.

Which translator supports more languages?

Language lists and feature coverage change. DeepL lists Arabic, English and French among its supported languages, while Google Translate also supports these languages. For other languages, and for voice, camera or offline features, check the provider's current list for the exact feature.

Can Google Translate translate Word and PDF files?

Google's desktop web document-translation feature supports Word, PDF, PowerPoint and Excel formats within its documented limits. Google says this feature is unavailable on smaller screens or mobile, and scanned image text may not be translated as document text.

Does DeepL support Arabic?

Yes. DeepL's current language list includes Arabic and identifies Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) as the Arabic variety available as a source and target language in Translator. Support for individual features such as glossaries can differ.

Is DeepL safe for confidential documents?

DeepL's terms differ between free and Pro services. Review the current policy, account type and your organization's requirements before submitting confidential or personal information. Apply the same review to any Google product you use.

Should I use machine translation for legal or medical documents?

Not as the final authority. Use machine translation only as an aid or draft, and have a qualified translator or subject-matter professional verify legal, medical, financial and safety-critical content.
