Legal AI tools: choose by legal workflow, not brand

Harvey, CoCounsel and Spellbook overlap, but they are not interchangeable. The practical decision is whether your bottleneck is broad matter work, research grounded in a legal information ecosystem, or contract drafting and review.

Editorial method: This is a task-based decision guide, not a controlled benchmark or universal ranking. Product capabilities, access and pricing can change. Confirm current details with each vendor and test on your own representative workflow.

The short answer

Harvey

Shortlist for larger legal teams evaluating a broad platform for research, drafting, document analysis and repeatable firm workflows.

CoCounsel

Shortlist when research, source-grounded analysis and workflows connected to Thomson Reuters legal content are central.

Spellbook

Shortlist for transactional teams whose recurring work is drafting and reviewing agreements in a document-centric workflow.

Compare the work

Legal research

Test whether answers cite primary authority, whether citations open to the exact passage, and whether the tool distinguishes binding authority from commentary.

Contract drafting and review

Give each product the same agreement and playbook. Check missed clauses, false positives, redline quality and whether suggested language preserves negotiated meaning.

Large matter analysis

Use a controlled document set with known facts. Measure extraction completeness, traceability and the effort needed to verify summaries.

Evaluation checklist

  • Use a synthetic or properly authorised sample matter; do not upload privileged or client information before written security and privacy approval.
  • Test the jurisdiction and practice area you actually serve. Product coverage and legal databases vary by market.
  • Require source-level verification, auditability, retention terms, access controls and a clear human approval step.
  • Compare total contract cost, implementation, training and integration—not only a quoted seat price.

Important limitation

  • AI output is not legal advice and can misstate law, omit controlling authority or invent citations. A qualified lawyer must verify every authority and approve all work product. Never use an unapproved consumer chatbot for privileged or confidential client material.
