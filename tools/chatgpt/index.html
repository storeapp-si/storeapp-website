ChatGPT: practical uses, strengths and limitations

ChatGPT is a general-purpose AI assistant for working through language, information and creative tasks. Its value depends less on asking clever questions and more on giving it a clear job, useful context and a way to check the result.

Best starting point: Drafting, explanations, brainstorming, structured problem-solving and working with supported files.

What ChatGPT is useful for

ChatGPT can help turn an unclear request into a plan, produce a first draft, explain a difficult concept, compare options or transform information you provide. Depending on the account, plan, region and enabled tools, it may also work with uploaded files, analyze data, interpret images, search the web or use specialized workflows. These capabilities are not identical for every user, so check the current product interface and official documentation.

Examples of practical workflows

  • Writing: Give it the audience, purpose, tone, required facts and length. Ask for a draft, then check every factual statement before publishing.
  • Learning: Ask for an explanation at a chosen level, followed by worked examples and a short quiz. Correct misconceptions against a trusted textbook or source.
  • Document work: Provide a permitted document and ask for a summary organized by section, open questions and quoted evidence. Verify quotations and page references against the original.
  • Data analysis: Upload a supported spreadsheet and define the question, columns and expected output. Check formulas, assumptions, missing values and calculations independently.
  • Planning: Ask for milestones, dependencies, risks and a checklist. Treat the output as a draft plan, not as proof that the assumptions are correct.

Where it can fall short

A fluent answer can still contain invented details, outdated information, weak reasoning or incorrect calculations. A citation or link is not proof that the linked source supports the claim. ChatGPT should not be treated as an authoritative legal, medical, financial or safety decision-maker. In high-impact work, use qualified human review and primary evidence.

It may also be the wrong choice when a task requires a deterministic specialist system, guaranteed real-time data, a fixed enterprise integration or a workflow that must meet strict audit requirements.

How to get better results

  1. State the outcome, audience and constraints.
  2. Provide only the context and files you are allowed to share.
  3. Specify the format: table, checklist, explanation, code review or decision memo.
  4. Ask it to separate verified facts, assumptions and unanswered questions.
  5. Check important claims against original sources and test any calculations or code.

A simple evaluation you can run

Choose three tasks you genuinely perform: one writing task, one research or explanation task, and one task involving a document or dataset. Use the same input and acceptance criteria each time. Record factual errors, omitted requirements, time spent correcting the output, ease of use and whether the result can be safely used in your workflow. Repeat on the plan you would actually pay for; feature access and usage limits can differ.

Privacy and account checks

Before uploading business, customer, employee or confidential material, review the data controls and terms for your account type. Check whether chat or file content may be used to improve models, available opt-out controls, retention settings, workspace administration and applicable contractual protections. Do not assume consumer and business accounts have identical controls.

ChatGPT compared with alternatives

Claude is worth testing for document-heavy drafting and analysis; Perplexity is designed around web research and source discovery; Microsoft Copilot may fit workflows centered on Microsoft services. The right choice is the one that completes your own tasks reliably, within your budget and privacy requirements.
